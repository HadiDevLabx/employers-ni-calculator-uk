# Employers NI Calculator UK — What an Employee Really Costs

> Employer National Insurance is 15% on everything above £5,000 per employee, so a £35,000 salary costs about £39,500 before pension.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

| Salary | Employer NI | Total cost before pension |
|---|---:|---:|
| £25,000 | £3,000 | £28,000 |
| £35,000 | £4,500 | £39,500 |
| £50,000 | £6,750 | £56,750 |

The secondary threshold of £5,000 is far lower than the employee's £12,570, so employer NI starts long before the employee pays anything.

The **Employment Allowance** lets eligible businesses reduce their annual employer NI bill, which matters most for small payrolls.

Large employers also pay the apprenticeship levy at 0.5%.

## What this does not cover

It excludes pension contributions, benefits, equipment and the cost of the space someone works in, all of which are real and often larger than the NI.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
